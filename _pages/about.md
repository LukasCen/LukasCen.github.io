I’m a Ph.D. student in Bioinformatics with a co-major in Statistics at North Carolina State University, where I work with David A. Rasmussen and Brian J. Reich. I develop statistical methods for learning about evolutionary and biomedical processes from biological data.

My research focuses on phylogenetics and phylodynamics. I’m interested in what evolutionary trees can tell us about how populations change over time and how genetic variation relates to differences in fitness. I draw on Bayesian hierarchical and spatial modeling to account for structure in the data and quantify uncertainty about the processes that generated it.

I’m also interested in simulation-based inference for biological models that are difficult to fit directly. I use simulations to test when a method can recover a known pattern and how sensitive its estimates are to the available data and modeling assumptions. These questions connect my interests in statistical genomics, infectious disease dynamics, and biomedical modeling.
